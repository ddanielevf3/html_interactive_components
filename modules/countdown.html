Live Countdown

wide support
Interactive module · reference sheet

A timer showing the time remaining at the moment the email is opened, generated as an image by an external service (it isn't a CSS animation). Works almost everywhere because, to the email client, it's "just" an image — the only constraint is that remote images need to load.

When to propose it: sales, flash sales, promotional deadlines — one of the safest modules to propose internally.

Fallback: no dedicated fallback is needed: where images are disabled, the alt text shows in place of the timer, just like any other image in the email.

supportedall major clients — it's technically an image
fallbackno client-specific gap: the only limit is the recipient having images disabled

Additional notes

What's actually configurable, and a real-world service that provides this out of the box.

What you can actually configure
  • Target date and time — calculated server-side, not via JavaScript in the client, so the remaining time is correct regardless of the recipient's timezone or local clock
  • Visual style — colours, fonts, size, and labels in the recipient's language (days/hours/minutes/seconds, or just hours/minutes for short countdowns)
  • What happens once it hits zero — the detail most often forgotten: without an "evergreen" fallback, anyone opening the email after the deadline sees the timer frozen at 00:00:00 (or broken). Proper services let you set an alternative image/state that the same URL serves automatically once the target date has passed (e.g. an "Offer ended" banner)
  • Multiple independent timers — in the same email (e.g. different deadlines per region or timezone)
Kickdynamic (now part of Litmus Personalize)
  • A purpose-built no-code service, acquired by Litmus in 2021 — generates the countdown as an animated GIF at the moment the email is opened, calculating the remaining time server-side
  • Part of a broader suite of "open-time" dynamic content that uses the same mechanism for other things: live inventory levels, weather- or location-based content, live polls, scratch-and-reveal
  • The practical benefit: no need to build or maintain the image-generation server yourself — configure the date, style and fallback in their dashboard and drop the resulting URL into the <img> tag

Since it's an image (usually a GIF), the same rules as the Animated GIF module apply: a descriptive alt for recipients with images disabled, and the same file-weight discipline.
